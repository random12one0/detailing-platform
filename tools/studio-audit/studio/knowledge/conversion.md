# What gets detailers booked (conversion know-how)

From the Studio's craft research. These are patterns that work for detailers, not rules of taste.

## What a customer needs to know, roughly in this order
1. Where they work, and whether they come to you (mobile) or you go to them (shop or drop-off). Answer this on
   the first screen; it is the first thing a customer filters on.
2. Which package fits their car and its state. Name who a package suits ("for a car that gets used every day").
3. The price for their vehicle size (read live from the dashboard; wording only points to it).
4. What is included, and how long it takes.
5. Condition surcharges explained honestly (pet hair, sand, heavy staining), so the quote does not surprise.
6. Real before-and-after photos of their own work.
7. Reviews, with where they came from.
8. For mobile work: what to have ready (water, power, a place to park), and the weather and cancellation policy.
9. A clear booking action, and for uncertain restoration jobs, a photo-assisted quote instead of a fixed price.

## Wording that helps
- The headline says what the customer gets, in their words: "Your car, done in your driveway." beats
  "Elevate your driving experience."
- The line under it answers the next question (where, how, how prices work).
- Section titles say what is in the section ("The menu, by vehicle size", "Where we work, and when").
- A booking title matches the booking mode: "Request a time" when the detailer confirms requests.
- Specific beats grand. Plain beats clever. Short beats long.

## Pattern sites (good to borrow from, not proof of conversion)
- Chase Detailing: playful type, driveway imagery, vehicle-size pricing, honest condition notes, a prominent
  booking path.
- Wash N Style: matches everyday problems to services, says who each package suits, shows price and duration,
  quotes ceramic coatings separately.
- Topaz Detailing: organises specialist care around enhance, protect, maintain; routes booking requests well.
