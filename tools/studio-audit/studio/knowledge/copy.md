# Copy rules (how the site talks)

## Andrew's rule
"Who's talking like this?" Write the way a straightforward local detailer would say it to a customer standing
in front of them. No reassurance, no explaining what the page already shows, no telling customers what they
already know, no marketing voice.

## Plain words
- Short sentences. Everyday words. Say the specific thing.
- No filler or hype: never "elevate", "seamless", "unleash", "transform your ride", "next level", "premium
  experience", "look no further", "we've got you covered", "passion", "meticulous attention to detail",
  "second to none", "state-of-the-art", "showroom shine" (unless the detailer said it).
- No exclamation marks. No emoji. No ALL CAPS sentences.
- No questions as headlines ("Looking for a detailer?").

## Facts never go in wording
- No digits, prices, percentages, currency signs, times, dates, counts or number words (two, ten, twenty-four,
  dozen, half, free, hundred, percent, dollar, buck). "Free" counts as a price claim, and so does "feel free":
  reword it.
- "one" is allowed as a pronoun ("the one place", "no one").
- Point to the fact instead: "Every price is on the menu below", "See the hours below".
- Do not name services, prices, towns or hours in wording; the dashboard-bound parts show them.

## Honest
- Say only what the detailer told us. No invented claims (insured, certified, eco, family-owned, years in
  business) unless the request says the detailer said so.
- Match the booking mode: requests are "Request a time", not "Book instantly".

## Lengths
- Small line above a headline: two to five words.
- Headline: up to about ten words.
- Line under a headline, intro: one or two sentences.
- Section title: up to about eight words.
