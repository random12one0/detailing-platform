## TASK ONE — give W16 a stated bound

**The ruling this rests on, and it is the ruling rather than the preamble:**

> **This product's booking surfaces are sized by data the detailer owns, so a
> rule phrased as "must fit" is not a rule at all — it is a hope about a number
> somebody else controls. A check cannot promise that and should not pretend
> to.**

**Why it is first:** `sweep-booking-steps.mjs` currently fails on step 1 for a
cause **no CSS change can honestly clear**. A gate failing for an unclearable
reason is worse than no gate, because it trains everyone to read a red as
weather.

**The measurement, already committed** — step 1 fits **five** services and the
demo tenant has six:

| services | 1440x900 | 392x844 |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | fits, ~22px spare | fits, ~29px spare |
| **6 (demo)** | **over 88px** | **over 71px** |
| 7 | over ~198px | over ~171px |

Card + gap is 110px at 1440, 100px at 392. `.bk-choices` is content-driven —
`min-height: auto`, no declared height, a flex column summing its children.
**There is no fixed card height that satisfies an unbounded service count.**

**Andrew's manager's two constraints, verbatim, and they are the difference
between this working and becoming the thing `docs/silences.md` exists to name:**

> The bound is DECLARED and committed, not computed at run time. The moment it
> derives itself from whatever the tenant happens to have, it becomes a gate
> that can never fail, which is the exact failure the expected-counts file was
> invented to stop — and you would have built the fifth instance of tonight's
> pattern while fixing the fourth.

> And the declaration has to name what it assumes out loud: this fits at 392
> with six services. Then a tenant with twelve is a known gap rather than a
> silent pass, and the next person reads the assumption instead of inferring it
> from a passing run.

**Done when:** the bound is a declared constant in the script, the gate is
seeded at it, it is proved **both directions** (a run where the bound holds and
a run where it is deliberately exceeded and the gate says so by name), and the
write-up says **the rule now promises something it can keep**.

## HELD, NOT FORGOTTEN — two decisions queued behind task one

Both are **held deliberately**, on instruction, because both change shape
depending on what a stated bound looks like in practice.

1. **The 104px scoping question.** `--bk-bar-clear` reserves 104px for a fixed
   bar and is overridden to 26px on pages that *have* one — so the full 104px
   applies **only to the four pages with no bar at all**. It is a deferred
   migration (`booking.css:115`), not a defect. Changing the default touches
   four surfaces a previous item deliberately excluded; a per-page override
   hides the decision inside an exception to the variable that exists to stop
   numbers drifting. **If a declared bound becomes the pattern, whether the
   padding sits inside or outside the bound may be the whole of this decision.**
2. **Whether the plans page's advisory 312px is worth anything.** It is
   `{ gate: false }` — advisory by design, prints `scrolls` not `over`, counted
   in no total. Real, largest overflow in the run, and not a failure.
