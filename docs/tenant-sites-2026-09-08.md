# The ten mock-ups — what each one is, and why it is not the others

**Built 2026-09-08 at his ask:** *"make my ten mock ups. All of them with, like,
slightly different styles ... It's basically a lot of variation. Like you said,
how ... you don't wanna show people two websites that look with small detailed
change because they won't see the differences, but with big differences."*

**Read this before opening any of them.** Each file's own header carries its
brief, its measured contrast and its reasoning; this is the map, so a session
does not have to open ten files to find out which one to look at.

They are served at `/example1` … `/example10` by `scripts/build-examples.mjs`,
which runs at the end of `npm run build`. The eleven older pages (`a-` … `k-`)
stay in `docs/tenant-sites/` and are **no longer served** — `a-shop`, `b-van`
and `c-volume` are the three he called *"very ai"*, and `d-` … `k-` all predate
any evidence of his taste.

---

## The variable he can see from across a room

Everything else is downstream of two choices: **what the ground is**, and
**whether the page moves**. No two share a ground. Four have no animation at
all, and they are spread through the running order rather than grouped, because
he clicks these in sequence and two still pages together would read as a broken
batch rather than a decision.

| # | file | ground | motion | the business |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `p-northlight` | a **photograph**, full bleed | moderate + **a mobile dock** | mobile detailing, Portland |
| 2 | `m-holloway` | **newsprint** stock, printed rule grid | **NONE** | a three-bay shop, 31 years old |
| 3 | `n-halo` | near-black made of **light** — glow + star field | **heavy**, scroll-linked | ceramic coating only, bay work |
| 4 | `o-rinsecity` | **painted ink**, halftone screen | almost none (hover only) | express wash, $34, 25 minutes |
| 5 | `q-meridian` | **warm cream**, page inside a rounded container | the quietest of the animated half | collection & delivery, high end |
| 6 | `r-railyard` | **blueprint grid**, set in mono | **NONE** | fleet cleaning, overnight, per unit |
| 7 | `l-tidewater` | a **dusk gradient sky** | **heavy**, scroll-linked | boats, RVs and cars; per-FOOT pricing |

## What each one exists to prove

**1 · `p-northlight` carries the best single idea in his whole batch.**
TASTE-NOTES § G: on a phone, `atelier` moves the photograph BELOW the headline
instead of squashing it, and pins a bar to the bottom — *Reviews · **Book
Now** · Services*. A mobile detailing customer's entire purpose is to book, and
on every other site in his eighteen that means scrolling back to the top. ~70px.
**No page in this repo had it.** It is also the test of § A — that a photograph
IS depth — so it carries almost no gradient furniture, one photographic ground,
and a scrim, which is exactly what `luster` gets wrong.

**2 · `m-holloway` is the argument that a still page can still have a ground.**
Every page he disliked scored near zero on motion, so a motionless page is a real
risk, taken deliberately because he asked for some with none. The pages he
called static failed on something ELSE as well — `rcd` puts a line icon where a
photograph belongs; `agautospa` has nine nav items and three CTA colours. So the
ground here is paper with a printed rule grid: depth without light. It is also
the densest of the ten, testing the other half of his sentence — that a page can
be *"a ton of information and words"* and still be easier to navigate than a
busy one, if the density is ordered like a catalogue.

**3 · `n-halo` answers his sentence literally.** *"good use of liek not
eveyhtin is just a box with text in it"* — `auxia`'s 5,362px ROUTED DIAGRAM,
rebuilt as the four stages of a coating job. A coating genuinely IS a sequence,
where each stage gates the next, so the device encodes something true rather
than decorating. The connectors draw as you scroll and **re-route vertically on
a phone**, which is what made the mechanic survive mobile (§ I).

**4 · `o-rinsecity` is the only one where the price is the hero.** Its whole
proposition is a number and a clock; leading with the work would be designing
against the client. The tension is *loud and cheap without becoming a discount
voucher* — so the copy underneath is dry, and there is no starburst, no
countdown, no exclamation mark.

**5 · `q-meridian` builds out the `atelier` reading**: the best detailer page in
his eighteen is LIGHT, not dark. Warm cream, the page inside a rounded container
with the ground showing at its edges, floating pill chips with a live status
dot, a review pill with an avatar stack, and a frosted translucent bar sitting
ON the photograph. Depth 27 — the highest of any detailer site he sent. That
combination had never been tried here, where every page was flat-dark or
editorial-serif.

**6 · `r-railyard` is the argument for why a platform must not ship one
template.** It sells to a fleet manager who wants a rate table, an SLA and an
invoice cadence — so it has no hero photograph and no testimonial about feeling
proud. **Every other mock-up would be actively wrong for that business, and it
would be wrong for the other nine.** `sixspeed` in his own list sells RV, marine
AND aviation off one page and reads as confused.

**7 · `l-tidewater` carries a price shape this product cannot express.**
`carolina` prices RV work per FOOT and sells trailer washouts and golf carts.
A set where every price is a flat number quietly asserts that every detailer
prices the same way. **It also carries a correction worth remembering**: its
first draft set the display in Fraunces, an elegant serif — squarely the family
he has rejected twice — and its own header argued that one type personality made
it fine. **A header that argues its way around a rule is how a page ends up
being the thing the research said to avoid.** Re-set in a condensed grotesque at
104px, which is what batch 1 § 3 actually found in his references.

**8 · `t-blackline` is the honest test of a corrected claim.** Batch 1 credited
*ground switching* as something his references share; batch 2 § 5 corrected it —
`rcd` alternates five grounds and he still called it plain. This page switches
ground harder than anything else in the ten and earns its depth from TYPE MASS
alone. **If it reads as flat, the correction was right.** No hero photograph on
purpose: film is invisible when done well, so a shiny-car shot is a photograph
of nothing.

**9 · `s-vera` refuses grids entirely** — one column, read top to bottom, which
is a difference visible from across a room in a set where everything else is
built on a grid. The customer is not admiring anything; they have a problem. So
the copy is diagnostic, and **the most interesting block on the page is what the
business says it CANNOT fix** — burns, long-term smoke, dye transfer, a live
leak. That disclaimer pattern is what every real detailer carries and no
AI-written page does.

**10 · `u-cedarchrome` lifts its main device from a page he DISLIKED.**
TASTE-NOTES § O records that `agautospa` — *"ehh"* — has one genuinely good
part: a masonry grid at staggered tile heights with a label bottom-left. **A
device is not guilty by association with the page it was found on**, and this is
the cleanest proof the analysis is reading mechanics rather than vibes.

## What every one of them carries

**The contract's twelve** (`docs/tenant-site-contract.md` § 2): the booking form
BUILT IN in that site's own design with its seven steps, the service catalogue,
the vehicle-size ladder, add-ons, monthly plans, the sale, gallery, reviews,
FAQ, how to pay, hours and area, identity.

**Every managed figure is tagged `data-live` with the table it comes from**, so
the difference between "this is placeholder" and "this comes from the dashboard"
is visible in the source rather than asserted in a comment.

**And each booking form's estimate is wired to demonstrate that THE SITE ASKS
AND NEVER COMPUTES** — the listener swaps in a starting figure, and on a live
copy it calls `calculate-booking` and prints what comes back. A site that adds
prices up itself will one day print a number the server does not charge.

**The data is real, adapted.** `docs/tenant-site-source-data-2026-09-08.md` —
ten live detailer sites read for their price ladders, disclaimers and FAQ
questions. The finding that changed every mock-up: **a real ladder has uneven
gaps.** `$200 / $220 / $350`, then `$220 / $250 / $375` — the oversized column
is a jump, not a step, and it reads as real precisely because it is
uncomfortable.

## Verified

Shot at **1440x900 and 392x844** and geometry-checked at both: nothing past the
right edge, no sideways scroll. Contrast **computed and quoted in each file's
header**, against the darkest AND lightest point a gradient ground puts behind
the text — and three tokens were caught failing that way before they shipped
(white on rinsecity's orange at 2.8:1, railyard's `--ctl` at 2.9:1,
cedarchrome's at 2.7:1); each is documented in its own file with what replaced
it.

**The geometry checker itself had a false positive worth keeping**: it flagged
seven of ten for elements past the right edge, all of them the booking
step-strip inside a horizontal scroller. A scroller's children are SUPPOSED to
sit past the viewport. It skips anything an ancestor clips now — the same
correction `sweep-widths.mjs` already carries, arrived at independently.
