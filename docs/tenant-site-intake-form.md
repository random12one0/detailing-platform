# The website intake form

The form a detailer fills in before their site is built. 2026-09-06.

The owner:

> *"I wanna have, like, a premade form ready for them to fill out... it has,
> like, a ton of questions. So think about, like, almost every scenario of the
> site... just have a question for each one as well as an option for custom
> notes, and at the end have, like, anything we missed write down here. But it
> should be very detailed, and that way they would almost not really have much
> to write down in the 'what did we miss' section. But that means that there's
> lots that you have to think about that might not be obvious."*

**The last sentence is the brief.** `docs/tenant-site-intake.md` is the short
version and its rule still holds; this is the long one, and the questions
worth having are the ones a detailer would not think to volunteer.

---

## How this is meant to work

**Every question has a notes box.** Not a general notes box at the end — one
per question, because *"we do offer that but only for regulars"* is an answer
to a specific question and is lost if it has to be remembered until the end.

**Nothing here is compulsory.** A detailer who answers half of it still gets a
site; the unanswered half is where we use judgement and say so.

**THREE RULES ABOUT WHAT IS NOT HERE:**

1. **Nothing the product already knows is asked.** Services, prices, hours,
   add-ons, promo codes and plans are in the dashboard. Asking again produces
   two answers and one of them will be stale. **The only exception is where
   the SITE needs it said differently from how the BOOKING PAGE needs it**,
   and each of those is marked.
2. **Nothing is asked that has one sane answer.** "Do you want it to work on a
   phone?" is not a question.
3. **Anything that would change what the site claims is asked twice**, once
   plainly and once as a consequence — insurance and guarantees especially.
   A site that claims something the detailer cannot back is worse than one
   that claims nothing.

## 1. The basics — and the ones that are not obvious

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 1.1 | **Business name, exactly as it should appear.** Including whether it is *LLC*, *Detailing* vs *Detail*, and any punctuation. | Every site gets this wrong once. It goes in the logo, the tab title, the footer, the emails and Google. |
| 1.2 | **How do you say it out loud?** | Only matters for one thing, and it matters a lot: it is the name people search for. *"AJ's"* vs *"A.J.'s"* changes what they type. |
| 1.3 | **Who is the person behind it — and do you want to be on the site?** | A one-person mobile business sells on the person far more than a shop does. Some detailers want their face and name on it; some deliberately do not, because they want to look bigger than one van. **Both are right and we cannot guess which.** |
| 1.4 | **Is there more than one of you?** | Changes "I" to "we" on every page, and changes whether the site can promise two cars in a day. |
| 1.5 | **How long have you been doing this?** | *"Detailing since 2019"* is the cheapest trust line on any site, and it is a fact nobody thinks to give us. |
| 1.6 | **Do you have a logo?** If yes, in what form — a real file, or a picture of one? | The difference between a usable logo and a JPEG screenshot of one is a week of back-and-forth. |
| 1.7 | **Is there an existing website?** What is right and what is wrong with it? | The fastest way to learn their taste, and the only way to learn what they have already been told by customers. |
| 1.8 | **Who owns the domain, and where is it registered?** | The single most common thing that stalls a launch. See §9. |

## 2. Where you work — and the edges of it

**This is the section detailers under-answer and customers care about most.**

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 2.1 | **Name the towns, suburbs or neighbourhoods you cover** — not a radius. | *"Within 25 miles"* means nothing to somebody who does not know where you start from. Names are also what people search. |
| 2.2 | **Where is the edge?** Is there somewhere just outside it that people always ask about? | Every mobile business has one, and a sentence about it saves a dozen phone calls. |
| 2.3 | **Do you charge for travel?** Past what point, and how much? | If yes, the site must say so. A travel fee revealed at the end is the most reliable one-star review in this trade. |
| 2.4 | **Do you have a shop, unit or driveway people can bring a car to?** | Changes the whole site: a mobile-only site should never show an address, and a drop-off business must. |
| 2.5 | **If mobile: what do you need when you arrive?** Water, power, a driveway, shade, a parking space? | **The most-skipped question and the one that ruins jobs.** A customer in an apartment block with no outdoor tap needs to know before they book, not when you arrive. |
| 2.6 | **Will you work in an apartment or condo car park?** In a public street? In a multi-storey? | Different answers per detailer and every one of them is a booking that either should or should not happen. |
| 2.7 | **Do you go to businesses — dealerships, fleets, offices?** | A completely different page and a different customer, and detailers often do it without mentioning it. |

## 3. What you actually do — the parts the dashboard does not hold

The catalog holds names, prices and durations. **The site needs the words
around them**, which is a different thing.

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 3.1 | **In one sentence, what makes yours different from the detailer three towns over?** | If they cannot answer this, the site cannot claim it, and we must not invent it. |
| 3.2 | **Is there a job you would rather not take?** | A site that quietly filters out the work they hate is worth more than one that brings in more of it. |
| 3.3 | **What is the job you WANT more of?** | Decides what goes at the top of the page. Often not the most popular one. |
| 3.4 | **Do you do ceramic coating, paint correction, or anything with a warranty?** | This changes the site structurally — those need their own explanation, their own photos and their own aftercare promise, and they hook into the maintenance-deadline feature. |
| 3.5 | **What products do you use, and do you want them named?** | Some customers search for a brand. Some detailers do not want to be tied to one. |
| 3.6 | **Do you do anything unusual?** Boats, motorbikes, RVs, engine bays, headlight restoration, pet hair, smoke odour. | **The long tail is where a small detailer wins**, and almost none of it appears on a services list. |
| 3.7 | **Are there jobs you price only after seeing the car?** | The site has to offer a quote path as well as a booking path, or those customers bounce. |
| 3.8 | **Roughly how long does your most common job take?** | Customers plan a day around it; the number exists in the dashboard but the site should say it in words. |
| 3.9 | **Do you do monthly or repeat plans?** *(In the product — asked only so the SITE knows whether to feature them.)* | A plan is a different sales page from a one-off. |

## 4. Proof — the half that actually sells

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | **How many before-and-after photos can you supply, and can we use them?** | The single most important content on a detailing site. **Nothing else converts as well** and nothing else is as often missing. |
| 4.2 | **Are the photos yours?** | A stock photo of somebody else's Porsche is spotted instantly and destroys the rest of the page. |
| 4.3 | **Do you have a Google Business Profile? Yelp? Facebook?** Links, please. | Reviews are the primary trust device for a business with no shopfront. |
| 4.4 | **How many reviews do you have, and what is the rating?** | Decides whether reviews go at the top of the page or halfway down. Three good ones is a section; forty is a headline. |
| 4.5 | **Can we quote reviews by name?** First name and initial, or anonymous? | |
| 4.6 | **Any certifications, training or brand accreditations?** | Ceramic installers especially — often a requirement of the warranty, and always worth a badge. |
| 4.7 | **Do you have video?** Even phone video. | |
| 4.8 | **Have you been in a local paper, a club, a car meet, anything?** | Detailers never think to mention this and it is free credibility. |

## 5. Trust, risk and the awkward questions

**This section exists because a website makes promises and somebody has to
keep them.** Every one of these is asked plainly and the answer is used
literally.

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 5.1 | **Are you insured?** By whom, and for how much? | **We will not put "fully insured" on a site without a yes.** It is the most common unearned claim in this trade and it is the one that gets a small business sued. |
| 5.2 | **Do you guarantee your work? What exactly do you promise?** | "Satisfaction guaranteed" means nothing and invites the worst reading. A specific promise is stronger and safer. |
| 5.3 | **What happens if something is damaged?** | If there is no answer, the site says nothing about it — which is better than a vague sentence a customer later quotes back. |
| 5.4 | **What if the customer is not happy?** Redo, partial refund, full refund? | |
| 5.5 | **What do you do about rain?** | Outdoor work in the open. Every mobile detailer has a policy and almost none of them have written it down. |
| 5.6 | **What if the car is much worse than described?** Pet hair, smoke, sand, a spill. | The site can set an expectation and save an argument in a driveway. |
| 5.7 | **Do you have a minimum charge?** | |
| 5.8 | **Do you work on brand-new cars, leases, wraps, PPF, matte paint?** | Matte and wrapped paint need different chemicals; a detailer who does not do them needs the site to say so. |

## 6. Money and policies

Some of this lives in the dashboard; **the site must say the same thing**, and
these questions are the ones that decide what it says.

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 6.1 | **When do you want to be paid?** Nothing up front, a deposit, or in full at booking. | Now a switch in the product (roadmap 2.20). The site has to explain whichever they chose. |
| 6.2 | **If a deposit: how much, and is it refundable?** | |
| 6.3 | **What is your cancellation policy?** How much notice, and what happens with less? | **Ask even if they say they do not have one** — everybody has one in practice, and writing it down is how it starts being honoured. |
| 6.4 | **What happens on a no-show?** | |
| 6.5 | **How can people pay?** Card, cash, Venmo/Zelle/Cash App, invoice, cheque? | Detailers take payments the internet does not know about and customers ask. |
| 6.6 | **Do you take tips?** Should the site mention it? | |
| 6.7 | **Do you do gift cards or gift certificates?** | Almost every detailer says no and then says *"actually, at Christmas"*. |
| 6.8 | **Any discounts you always honour?** Military, first responder, senior, student, repeat. | Rarely in the promo table because they are given verbally, and they are exactly what people search for. |
| 6.9 | **Do you price differently for large vehicles?** | In the product, asked here so the site sets the expectation before the price does. |

## 7. How customers reach you

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1 | **What is the best way to reach you — and the way you do NOT want?** | A site that puts a phone number on every page belongs to somebody who wants calls. Some detailers hate the phone. |
| 7.2 | **Should your phone number be on the site, or only the booking form?** | |
| 7.3 | **Should your email be on the site?** | **The owner's answer, 2026-09-06: it is the detailer's choice, so it is a question rather than a policy.** |
| 7.4 | **Do you want to be textable?** | This trade runs on text; some detailers want it and some will not give out a personal mobile. |
| 7.5 | **How fast do you normally reply?** | *"Usually within an hour"* is a promise worth making if it is true, and a complaint generator if it is not. |
| 7.6 | **Which social accounts should be linked?** And which exist but should NOT be linked? | The second half matters — a dormant Facebook page with one 2019 post makes a business look closed. |
| 7.7 | **Do you want a contact form as well as booking?** | Two different intents: "book me in" and "I have a question". |

## 8. What it should feel like

**This is the section the owner has not settled yet** (question 0), so the
questions here gather material rather than make decisions.

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 8.1 | **Name two or three websites — any trade — that you like the look of.** | The fastest and most reliable taste signal there is. |
| 8.2 | **Name one you do not like, and say why.** | Usually more informative than the likes. |
| 8.3 | **Do you have brand colours already?** From a wrap, a business card, a logo. | A van livery is a brand nobody thought to mention. |
| 8.4 | **Dark or light?** | Detailing sites skew dark; it is not universal. |
| 8.5 | **Serious and premium, or friendly and local?** | These are two different sites, and both are correct businesses. |
| 8.6 | **Is there wording you use that must stay?** A tagline, a phrase, a way you describe the work. | |
| 8.7 | **Is there anything you must NOT say?** A word you hate, a claim you will not make, a competitor's phrase. | |

## 9. The practical things that stall a launch

**Every one of these has delayed a real website.**

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 9.1 | **Do you own a domain?** Which registrar, and do you have the login? | |
| 9.2 | **If not, what would you like it to be?** Two or three options. | Their first choice is usually taken. |
| 9.3 | **Is there an existing site we would be replacing, and who controls it?** | An old site nobody can take down will outrank the new one. |
| 9.4 | **Do you have a business email on that domain?** | Changing a domain's records can break email, and that is not a surprise anybody should get. |
| 9.5 | **Is your Google Business Profile claimed and verified?** | The most valuable free thing a local business owns, and often unclaimed. |
| 9.6 | **Is the address, phone and name identical everywhere they appear online?** | Inconsistency here quietly costs local search ranking. |
| 9.7 | **Do you have anything already written?** Flyer text, an Instagram bio, a Facebook description. | Their own words beat ours. |
| 9.8 | **Who else needs to approve this?** A partner, a spouse, a business partner. | Finding out at the end is how a finished site gets rebuilt. |

## 10. Growth, briefly

| # | Question | Why it is asked |
|---|---|---|
| 10.1 | **Where do your customers come from today?** Word of mouth, Instagram, Google, a sign on the van. | Decides what the site optimises for. |
| 10.2 | **What do you want more of that you are not getting?** | |
| 10.3 | **Do you run ads?** | Changes whether the site needs a landing page separate from the home page. |
| 10.4 | **Is there a season?** | A site that says nothing in February when the work is in June is a wasted quarter. |
| 10.5 | **Do you want an email list?** | The product can do it; some detailers will never use it. |

---

## 11. Anything we missed

> **Is there anything about your business, your customers or the way you work
> that we have not asked about and should have?**
>
> **And: is there anything on this form you were not sure how to answer?**

**The second question is the more useful of the two**, and it is the one that
makes this form get better. A question that is regularly left blank or
answered *"not sure"* is a badly worded question, and it should be rewritten
after the first three detailers rather than after the thirtieth.

---

## What happens after it is filled in

**Sections 1, 2, 5, 6 and 7 are facts and are used literally.** Sections 3, 4,
8 and 10 are material for judgement.

**The one rule on the other side of the form: nothing on the finished site may
claim anything this form did not establish.** Not insured unless 5.1 said so,
no guarantee beyond 5.2's words, no service area wider than 2.1, no review
quoted that 4.5 did not allow. **A website that promises on a detailer's
behalf is a liability wearing a design.**
