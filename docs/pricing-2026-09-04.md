# What to charge — 2026-09-04

He asked for this properly rather than in passing:

> *"Can we actually spend some time to think about pricing? … Do you think a
> thousand dollars is a good base price? Should I charge less? Charge more? I
> kinda wanna be like 'hey, this is a really good deal' … I want it to instantly
> lock in that this isn't just every other thing, this is something that's
> really valuable."*

**The headline, before any of the detail: he is not charging too much. He is
charging too little, and the fix for "make it feel like a good deal" is not a
lower number — it is showing what the alternative costs.**

---

## What the page charges today

| | Setup | Monthly | Annual |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Website + dashboard** (list) | **$999** (was $900 until 2026-09-04) | $60 | $600/year |
| **Website + dashboard** (founding, 3 spots) | $499 | $40 | — |
| **Booking only** | none | $35 | — |

`app/src/landing/pricing.js`. The founding CAP is not in that file on purpose —
it is counted from the accounts themselves, so the page can never advertise a
spot that is already taken.

## What the field charges

**Software alone, no website:**

| | Monthly |
|---|---|
| Zenbooker | **$29** (booking-first, closest in shape to ours) |
| Jobber | **$29–$199** |
| Urable *(detailing-specific)* | **from $45** |
| Housecall Pro | **$59–$299** |
| Mobile Tech RX *(detailing-specific)* | **$39–$199** |

**A custom website, one-off:**

| | Cost |
|---|---|
| Freelancer, basic custom site | **$500–$5,000** |
| Freelancer, professional site | **$2,000–$8,000** |
| Agency | **$10,000–$35,000** |
| **Ongoing upkeep of a custom site** | **$50–$200/month** |

## Question 1 — is $999 a good base price?

**Yes, and it is at the bottom of the range for what it is.** A hand-built
custom site starts at $500 from a freelancer and $2,000 for anything
professional. He is charging $999 for a custom site **plus** the booking
software **plus** the dashboard **plus** ongoing changes.

**Do not go lower.** The risk of a lower number is not lost margin — it is
signal. A detailer deciding between a $999 quote and a $3,000 agency quote reads
the gap as a difference in seriousness, and he has no case studies yet to argue
otherwise. **Below about $750 the price starts doing the arguing for the wrong
side.**

**Do not go higher yet either.** He has no portfolio, no testimonials and no
track record, and the founding offer exists precisely to buy those. **Raise the
list price after three sites exist to point at** — that is a decision with
evidence behind it, and today's is not.

**The number is fine. The FRAMING is what is missing**, and that is the real
answer to *"I want it to feel like a really good deal"*:

- **Put the alternative next to it.** An agency site is $10,000+. A freelancer
  is $2,000–$8,000 and does not include booking software. Wix or Squarespace is
  cheaper but it is **his** evening for the rest of time, and it does not come
  with a dashboard that takes bookings.
- **Price the thing nobody else includes.** *"Ongoing changes"* is his real
  differentiator and it is currently invisible. **Ongoing upkeep of a custom
  site is $50–$200/month on its own** — more than his entire monthly fee. Saying
  that out loud does more for perceived value than any discount.
- **The landing page already does half of this** (*"not the thousands an agency
  charges — then charges again every time a price changes"*). It should do it
  with numbers.

## Question 2 — keep $60/month?

**Yes, and it is underpriced.** Housecall Pro is $59 for software alone with no
website. Urable, detailing-specific, is $45 for software alone. Zenbooker is $29
for booking alone.

**His $60 includes all of that plus hosting a custom site plus making changes to
it whenever the detailer asks** — where the last item alone benchmarks at
$50–$200/month.

**Leave it.** There is room above it later, and the moment to use that room is
when a founding customer renews rather than now.

## Question 3 — is $600/year the right annual price?

> *"How much is sixty times twelve? So they're saving a hundred and twenty
> dollars… Is that a good amount to be saved?"*

**$60 × 12 = $720. Paying $600 saves $120, which is 16.7% — exactly two months
free.**

**That is the industry standard, precisely.** The most common SaaS annual
discount is **17%, framed as "2 months free"**; the normal band is **15–20%**;
**below 15% does not move anyone**, and **above 30% signals the monthly price
was inflated.** He landed on the textbook number by accident.

**Change nothing except the words.** *"Get 2 months free"* converts better than
*"Save $120"* or *"Save 17%"* even though all three are the same — the
months-free framing is easier to picture. **The page should say months, not
dollars.**

## Question 4 — how many founding spots?

> *"Maybe I can make it only just my first person or first two, or does three
> sound good?"*

**Three. Keep it.**

- **One is not an offer, it is an anecdote.** Nobody feels urgency about a deal
  with one seat, and he cannot say "our founding customers" about one person.
- **Three gives him three references, three different businesses to learn
  from,** and three genuinely different sites for the portfolio the list price
  depends on.
- **Three is also the point where the platform pays for itself.** Fixed costs
  are ~$45/month once this is live; three founding customers at $40 is $120.
- It is already what the database defaults to, and the count is computed rather
  than typed, so nothing has to change.

**Scarcity only works if it is true.** When the third spot goes, the offer
closes and the price becomes $999/$60. **If he later wants more founding
customers, that is a new offer with a new name — never quietly raising the cap**,
because the page says how many are left and somebody will notice.

## Question 5 — the month-to-month price

He wants three ways to pay, with month-to-month costing more because it carries
no commitment.

**A clean ladder, and the numbers almost pick themselves:**

| | Effective monthly | Yearly |
|---|---|---|
| **Annual, paid up front** | **$50** | $600 |
| **Annual, paid monthly** *(12-month term, exit fee)* | **$60** | $720 |
| **Month to month** *(cancel any time, no fee)* | **$75** | $900 |

**$75 is a 25% premium over the committed price**, which sits inside the normal
20–30% band for a no-commitment option. **$79 is 32%** — defensible, at the top
of the range, and it applies his own *"things that end in ninety nine feel more
professional"* instinct. **Either works; $75 is the safer of the two** because
it keeps the whole ladder in round numbers a detailer can do in their head.

**Founding equivalents:** $400 up front / $40 monthly / $50 month-to-month.

**And the middle option must NOT be pre-selected**, however obviously it is the
one most people should take. Pre-selecting the committed plan is the first thing
the FTC named in its Adobe complaint (see the payments research).

## Question 6 — booking-only

> *"On the booking-page-only they get this whole admin dashboard and booking
> page. So should we have that cost a little more, or at least maybe have like a
> one-time payment upfront?"*

**Two things are true and they pull in opposite directions.**

At $35 against $60, **the website plan is only $25/month more**, which makes it
an easy upgrade — that is good, and it is why the gap should stay small. But
$35 for software alone is **above Zenbooker at $29** and near Urable at $45,
without a website to justify it.

**What actually separates them should be the ongoing-changes promise, not the
price.** Booking-only is the product; website plus dashboard is the product
*plus him*. **State that plainly and the $25 gap sells itself.**

**On a setup fee for booking-only: a small one is defensible and it is not
really about money.** Setting up somebody's services, hours, prices and travel
areas is real work he will do on a call. **A $99 setup would filter out the
people who sign up and never finish** — which is the expensive customer, not the
cheap one. **But it also puts a wall in front of the easiest sale he has**, and
booking-only is the on-ramp that turns into a website customer later.

**Recommendation: leave booking-only at $35 with no setup fee for now, and
revisit after the first three.** It is the one price with no evidence behind it
in either direction, and it is the cheapest to change later because nobody is on
it yet.

---

## What to actually do

**Change nothing about the numbers except adding one.**

1. **Keep $999 / $60 / $600 / $35.** All four are defensible against the field
   and three of them are already close to optimal.
2. **Add month-to-month at $75** (or $79), and **make annual-paid-monthly the
   visual middle without pre-selecting it.**
3. **Say "2 months free" instead of "$120 less".**
4. **Keep three founding spots**, and close the offer when they are gone.
5. **Put the comparison on the page** — the agency number, the freelancer
   number, and the $50–$200/month that ongoing site changes cost everywhere
   else. **That is what makes it read as a good deal**, not a smaller price.

**And the one thing worth more than all of the above: the ongoing-changes
promise is his differentiator and it is currently almost invisible.** Nobody
else in the comparison table will edit a detailer's website because they asked.
