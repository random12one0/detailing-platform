# Where to go and look at websites — 2026-09-07

**His ask:**

> *"Can you do some research and find me places where I could look at car
> detailing websites? Like, there's just a mass link so I could just go through
> and pick ones that I like. Maybe there's even a better one where they're
> actually ranked… or maybe there's Discord communities, Reddit posts, a Reddit
> server, a website — any place where I could look at some really well designed
> websites."*

**What this is for.** Roadmap 9.1 — the gallery, which is the one thing
blocking the intake form, and which only he can fill because it is his taste.
**What is needed per entry is small: a link and one line on what he likes about
it.** Ten to twenty is plenty.

**Read the tiers as different jobs rather than as a ranking.** Tier 1 is what a
good site in HIS trade looks like. Tier 2 is what expensive craft looks like in
any trade. They answer different questions and the second one is the more
dangerous to copy — see § 5.

## 2. Many versions of the same trade, fast

Templates are the quickest way to see twenty takes on one brief. **They are not
taste references** — a template is designed to suit everybody, which is exactly
the averaged look this whole exercise is trying to avoid — but they are very
good for spotting *"I keep liking the ones that do X."*

- **[Webflow templates — car detailing](https://webflow.com/templates/search/car-detailing-e3be6)** and **[auto detailing](https://webflow.com/templates/search/auto-detailing-0f476)** — live previews, no account needed
- **[Webflow Inspo — 7 best car detailing templates](https://webflow.com/list/car-detailing)** — the shortlist of the above
- **[Framer marketplace — Motorflow](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/motorflow/)** — the automotive one people point at
- **[Dribbble — /tags/car-detailing](https://dribbble.com/tags/car-detailing)** — 400+, but **mostly pictures of designs rather than working sites**; good for a colour or a layout idea, bad for judging whether something actually works
- **[Behance — car detailing website projects](https://www.behance.net/search/projects/Car%20Detailing%20website)** — same caveat, longer case studies

## 4. Communities

Honest answer: **there is no active Discord or subreddit dedicated to good car
detailing websites.** The closest useful things are:

- **[r/web_design](https://www.reddit.com/r/web_design/)** — 140k+, and its
  critique threads are the useful part: people post a site and get told what is
  wrong with it, which is more instructive than a gallery of winners
- **[r/AutoDetailing](https://www.reddit.com/r/AutoDetailing/)** — not a design
  community, but **the place where detailers say what they actually want from a
  website**, which is a different and useful signal
- **[r/UI_Design](https://www.reddit.com/r/UI_Design/)** and
  [r/graphic_design](https://www.reddit.com/r/graphic_design/) — general craft
- **[Sidebar.io](https://sidebar.io/)** and
  [Web Designer News](https://www.webdesignernews.com/) — a short list a day by
  email, so it comes to him instead of him going looking

## 5. The one warning worth reading before clicking anything

**Two of these tiers will pull in opposite directions, and the pull is the whole
reason `docs/TASTE-NOTES.md` exists.**

The five sites he sent unprompted on 2026-09-07 were, in his own trade, close to
what a marketing page normally is — a real photograph at scale, a huge
condensed headline, one loud colour, and the page switching from black to white
between sections. **Not novel. Confident and well made.** That file's § 4 puts
it plainly: *what separates them is craft and confidence, not novelty.*

**Awwwards and Godly reward the opposite.** They are judged on ambition, so the
work that wins is often work no detailing customer would tolerate — a page that
takes six seconds to explain itself to somebody who wants to know whether their
truck can be done on Saturday.

**So: Tier 1 for what the site should BE, Tier 3 for how well it should be
MADE.** And the trade listicles in Tier 1 are agency marketing — the sites they
praise are frequently their own clients — so they are a place to look, never a
verdict.

**And the thing to write down is not the site.** It is the one line about why.
*"I like how big the price is"* is worth more than the URL, because the URL only
says what he liked and the sentence says what to do next time.
