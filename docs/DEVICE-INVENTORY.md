# The device inventory — what "there needs to be MORE" actually means

**Written 2026-09-09.** His complaint, and it is the whole reason this file
exists:

> *"It doesn't have those elements that professionally made websites have… it
> was very bland, **there wasn't much to it. There needs to be more in the
> website, different features and whatnot** that are in these good websites that
> this one just didn't have. It was like very basic."*

**"Different features and whatnot" is a real, listable thing, and every item
below was seen on a site HE picked.** Nothing here is invented; every entry
names the site it came from and cites `docs/TASTE-NOTES.md`.

---

## THE FLOOR

**A page ships with TEN OR MORE of these.** The ten he rejected carry between
two and four each, which is the measurable form of "very basic".

**The floor is a floor, not a shopping list.** Ten chosen because they suit the
subject beats fifteen bolted on. Every entry below has a **when NOT to** line
and it is the more important half — a device used where it does not belong is
the "crowded" complaint he made about `chicagoautopros`.

**And the tenth device is not the point — the FIRST one is.** The page needs one
object at a scale nothing else on it approaches. See D1.

---

## A · THE ALWAYS-ONS

Not optional. A page without these is not thin, it is broken.

### A1 · A ground that is not a flat fill
**What:** gradient, glow, texture, canvas or photograph. `TASTE-NOTES` § E rule 7.
**Why:** *"basic and flat isn't as inviting"* — his own words, twice.
**When NOT to:** never. There is no case for a flat fill.

### A2 · A real photograph, above the fold
**What:** a car, at scale — cut out, full-bleed, or inside a large rounded card.
**Where it came from:** the first line of § 3, what all five of his batch-1 picks
share.
**When NOT to:** never. **All ten rejected pages had zero photographs, and the
eleven before them all had some** — this is the regression
`tenant-sites-diagnosis-2026-09-09.md` § 1 found.

### A3 · A scrim under text on a photograph
**What:** gradient blend or frosted layer. `atelier` blends its Tesla into the
cream ground so there is no hard edge.
**When NOT to:** never, when text sits on an image. Both sites he called bad
fail this.

### A4 · The most important number, not at body size
**What:** the price. `meli`'s are **14px cyan** — the most commercially important
figure on the page, set at body size — and it is on his favourite site.
**When NOT to:** never.

## B · IDENTITY — cheap, and they do the most work

### B1 · The accent-final-word heading
**What:** every heading in white with the **last word or two in the accent** —
*GET YOUR **FREE QUOTE***, *WHY LUXURY DETAILING **MATTERS***. From `meli`,
§ P.
**Why it is first on this list:** no new colour, no new face, no new layout, and
it makes a heading recognisably theirs. **The cheapest identity device in either
batch.**
**When NOT to:** when it cannot be EVERY heading. The discipline is the device;
applied to some, it reads as an accident.

### B2 · The giant bleeding footer wordmark
**What:** the business name at ~200px on flat accent, bleeding off the right
edge, last letters stepped down in tone so it fades out of frame. Footer links
at ~10px mono underneath. From `auxia`, § P.
**Why:** *"it makes the page END rather than merely stop — a thing none of our
ten mock-ups do and every one of them could."*
**When NOT to:** when the name is long enough to wrap. It is the word used as a
shape, not a logo.

### B3 · The outlined wordmark behind the hero
**What:** an enormous outlined name sitting BEHIND the hero content. From
`MOXOM`, § A.
**When NOT to:** on the same page as B2 — one oversized wordmark, not two.

## C · TRUST, and it belongs where the decision happens

### C1 · The floating availability pill with a live dot
**What:** *"Available Today"* with a green dot, floating over the hero. From
`atelier`.
**Why:** it answers the mobile-detailing customer's first question before they
scroll.
**When NOT to:** when nothing makes it true. A hard-coded "Available Today" is a
lie the booking engine can contradict — drive it from `available-slots`.

### C2 · The review pill with an avatar stack
**What:** a floating pill carrying overlapping avatars and *4.9 · 1,250+
reviews*. From `atelier`.
**When NOT to:** when the detailer has few reviews. Three reviews in an avatar
stack advertises three reviews.

### C3 · The frosted trust bar ON the photograph
**What:** a translucent bar sitting over the hero image carrying two or three
proof points. From `atelier`.
**When NOT to:** when it would cover the subject of the photo. **Contrast is
measured against the LIGHTEST part of the image behind it**, not against the
ground.

### C4 · Review proof INSIDE the form
**What:** the rating strip at the bottom of the booking card, at the moment of
commitment — not scattered around the page. From `meli`'s quote page, § M, and
flagged there as **the one difference clearly worth copying.**
**When NOT to:** never, if reviews exist.

### C5 · The stat strip
**What:** a row of figures — *7K · 3K · 5K*, *24+ · 1.2K+ · 35 · 99%*. **Four of
his five batch-1 picks carry one**, and § 3 calls it *"the single easiest thing
to copy."*
**When NOT to:** when the numbers are small or invented. A new detailer's real
numbers are worse than none.

## D · STRUCTURE — this is where the thinness actually lives

### D1 · ONE object at a scale nothing else approaches
**What:** `fora`'s product window rising into frame; `auxia`'s 90px headline
against 14px body; `auxia`'s 200px footer word. § P generalises it: **a page
wants one object at a scale nothing else on it approaches, and where it sits is
free.**
**When NOT to:** two of them. Then neither is large.

### D2 · The routed connector diagram
**What:** right-angled connector lines with rounded corners routing between
floating pills and soft-shadowed cards, a mono micro-label sitting ON a
connector. **Nothing in a grid — drawn as a system, and it draws itself as you
scroll.** From `auxia`, the 5,362px section, **and § C says this is the thing he
was pointing at.**
**Transfers to:** the detailing PROCESS, or the booking flow, **instead of three
cards.**
**When NOT to:** when the process has fewer than four steps, or on a page that
already has a heavy motion device. It is expensive on a mid-range phone —
measure on a throttled CPU.

### D3 · Dotted connectors between icon tiles
**What:** a horizontal row of icon tiles joined by dotted lines. From `authkit`.
**The cheap version of D2** when the page cannot afford the diagram.
**When NOT to:** alongside D2.

### D4 · The page inside a rounded container
**What:** the whole page in a rounded shell with the ground showing at its
edges. From `atelier`.
**When NOT to:** with a full-bleed hero — they fight.

### D5 · The staggered masonry photo grid
**What:** offset tiles rather than an even grid. From `agautospa`, § O — **the
good part of a page he rated "ehh"**, which is the point: take the device, leave
the page.
**When NOT to:** with fewer than five real photographs. Masonry with four tiles
is a grid with a mistake in it.

### D6 · The two-column tick list
**What:** *"THIS PACKAGE INCLUDES:"* then a two-column list of ticks — *Front
bumper · Full hood · Headlights · Mirror caps*. **Ticks, not prose.** From
`chicagoautopros`, § P.
**Maps straight onto `services.features`**, which the contract already requires
a site to print and **which all ten mock-ups render as a prose `<small>`.**
**When NOT to:** for a single service with no discrete parts — a prose line
suits a detail, a tick list suits a package.

### D7 · The price table tabbed by vehicle type
**What:** Cars / SUVs / Trucks / Bikes as tabs over a price table. From `MOXOM`,
§ A — **and § A notes this is exactly the ladder this product already has** in
`vehicle_size`.
**When NOT to:** when the tenant prices one size only.

## E · THE ONE THAT MATTERS MOST

### E1 · The portfolio row
**What:** one completed job as a SINGLE object — the photograph with a corner
badge naming the package; the car and package (*"Porsche 911 Turbo | Meli's
Signature Detail"*); one line of what was actually done; **a Book Now button on
the row itself**; and under a hairline, that customer's real Google review in
italic with their name, city, the Google mark and five stars.

From `meli`, § L, which calls it **"the single most transferable thing found so
far"**: *a gallery, a service menu, a testimonial and a booking CTA collapsed
into one object. Every other site in the eighteen — ours included — spends four
separate sections on those four jobs.*

**AND THIS PRODUCT CAN GENERATE IT.** `job_photos` for the image,
`booking_services` for the package, the vehicle for the title, the customer's
first name and city, the detailer's review link. **A completed job becomes a
portfolio row with no typing** — a thing no website builder can do, because none
of them knows what work was done.

**When NOT to:** on a brand-new tenant with no completed jobs. It degrades to
the service menu until there is work to show.

## F · PHONE

### F1 · The floating bottom dock
**What:** a rounded bar fixed to the bottom of the screen — *Reviews*, a black
**Book Now** pill, *Services*. From `atelier`, and § G calls it **"the single
best idea in either batch"** and *"the one I would steal first."*
**Why:** a mobile detailing customer's entire purpose is to book. On every other
site in the eighteen that means scrolling back to the top. **This costs about
70px.** None of our pages do it.
**When NOT to:** never, on a phone.

### F2 · The photograph moves BELOW the text
**What:** on the desk the headline sits over the car; on a phone the headline
gets clean ground at the top and the car fills the lower two-thirds, blended in.
From `atelier`, § G. **A different layout, not a squeezed one.**
**When NOT to:** when the hero is not a photograph.

## THE FAILURE THIS INVENTORY IS AGAINST

`meli` is his **favourite** detailer site and he still called it *"lacking a
bit"*. § D says exactly why, and it is the definition of thin:

> *"The page never changes ground, never changes rhythm, never gets big, and
> never has any light in it."*

And on a phone it gets worse: five near-identical cards, **~2,400px of scrolling
through the same shape**, with nothing changing but the photograph and the
price, and no persistent way to book.

**That is the target. Not "avoid looking generic" — "do not be thin."**

## G · OUR OWN LANDING PAGE'S MECHANICS — added 2026-09-08, at his instruction

**He reopened this source himself:** *"especially our detailingplatform.com
landing page, I really like the look of our website, just how it flows and
animations and whatnot… those cool kind of features that a normal website won't
have, like the glow around your mouse."* (`TASTE-NOTES` § BATCH 3.)

**THE MECHANICS CROSS OVER. THE SKIN NEVER DOES** — no Archivo, no JetBrains
Mono, no `#0B0D0E`, no accent green, no section order. All eight are read out of
`app/src/landing/thread.js`, which is **read-only to this session.** Reimplement
them in the tenant page's own file; do not import from `app/src`.

### G1 · The cursor glow on the ground
A soft light following the pointer, **lerped at 0.09 so it lags slightly** —
*"the small lag is the difference between a lit surface and a torch taped to the
mouse."* One composited layer moved by transform. **The device he named by
name.**
**When NOT to:** on a phone (no cursor), or under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Both
already guarded by `FINE && !LITE`.

### G2 · The weighted scroll
Wheel distance ×1.22 eased at 0.055 toward a banked target. **Thirty lines
instead of a 3 KB library, and it moves the real scroll position** so
`position: sticky` keeps working. `?smooth=0` turns it off.
**When NOT to:** touch devices — a finger scroll is the thing phones get right.

### G3 · `data-glow` — the pointer hotspot on a card
Card tracks `--mx`/`--my` and lights under the cursor. Cheap, no rAF.
**When NOT to:** on more than one KIND of surface per page.

### G4 · `data-tilt` — 5° tilt with a travelling highlight
Rotation plus a light position, one rAF, reset on `pointerleave`.
**When NOT to:** on anything containing a form field or a photograph's subject.

### G5 · The pinned beat with scrubbed progress
A section holds still while its contents advance on scroll progress, and the
scroll gets **heavier inside the beat (×0.5)** and light again on the way out.
**When NOT to:** more than twice on a page, and never on a beat under one screen
tall.

### G6 · The number that rolls up
A figure counts to its value when it enters frame, on an eased curve.
**Pairs with C5, the stat strip.**
**When NOT to:** on a price. A price that animates reads as a price that moves.

### G7 · Reveal-on-scroll with a per-element progress variable
`--rp` / `--ep` set per element rather than a binary class, so the reveal can be
scrubbed rather than triggered. **The hidden state is added by SCRIPT, never by
the stylesheet** — a failed script must not leave a blank page.

### G8 · Parallax by `--py`
Elements offset against scroll by a small factor. **Cheapest depth device
available**, and depth is the requirement.
**When NOT to:** on text somebody has to read while it moves.

## H · THREE DEFECTS FROM SITE `v-goldenhour`, PASS 1 — 2026-09-08

All three were invisible in the source and obvious in a screenshot. Two of them
would have shipped under a comment claiming a pass.

### H1 · A CONTRAST FIGURE COMPUTED FROM THE TOKEN TABLE CAN BE A LIE
The Book button was written `color:var(--bone)` on `background:var(--ink)` and
the calculator duly reported **16.13:1**. It rendered at about **2.4:1**, because
`.nav a` is (0,1,1) and `.navbook` was (0,1,0) — **the cascade handed it
`--ink-2`, grey on near-black.** The ratio was correct about the intent and
wrong about the page.
**The rule that follows:** the existing repo rule *"a contrast comment is written
AFTER the calculator runs"* is necessary and NOT sufficient. **A ratio for text
inside a nested selector must be read off the RENDERED page**
(`getComputedStyle`), not off the token it was assigned.

### H2 · "A PHOTOGRAPH AT SCALE" IS A CLAIM ABOUT THE FOLD, AGAIN
Site `k-cedar` recorded this exact failure and it recurred anyway: the headline
at `clamp(52px,10.4vw,152px)` pushed the hero photograph to **640px down a 900px
screen**, so a page whose whole subject is a photograph opened on type and empty
ground. Fixed at 112px max and 17ch, photo top now **432px**.
**Measure it:** the hero image's `getBoundingClientRect().top` must be under half
the viewport height. This is an ABSOLUTE with a right answer, which is the only
kind of judgement worth trusting here.

### H3 · A DEVICE THAT FITS ON THE DESK CAN RUN OFF ITS OWN CONTAINER ON A PHONE
C3, the frosted trust bar ON the photograph, stacked to four rows at 392 and its
button fell off the bottom edge of the image. **F2's rule is that the phone gets
a DIFFERENT layout, not a squeezed one** — so below 760px the bar leaves the
photograph entirely and becomes a solid card on the ground beneath it.
**Generalises:** every device positioned `absolute` inside a fixed-ratio box owes
a phone answer, and "it wraps" is not one.
