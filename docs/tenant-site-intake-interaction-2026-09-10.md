# Every question in the website brief, asked a better way

2026-09-10. His brief, second time of asking, because the first answer was
seven general mechanisms and that was not it:

> *"When I meant by brainstorming ideas is not general, but very specific to
> each question. Like, hey, analyse this question — could this be more visual,
> more interactive to answer it? For example, the corners one: instead of just
> asking round or square corners and you tap words, it actually shows visual.
> The colours, there's this colour widget where you can choose colours, there's
> a range, type in hex codes. There's obviously a lot of questions, so some of
> those questions could be better interactive, or shown in a different way
> other than just typing some buttons or typing in text."*

**So: one row per question. What it is now, and whether there is a better
control.** Questions where a chip is already the right answer are marked and
left alone — there are 31 of them and pretending otherwise would be padding.

**Status:** ✅ built · ▶ worth building · — a chip is right

## 1 · The look — where a control can do the most

**This is where his corners example belongs, and it generalises.** A visual
either/or is one tap, needs no reading, and settles something that would
otherwise take a paragraph nobody can write. There are **ten** of these
available, not three.

| # | Now | Better |
|---|---|---|
| H1 movement | ✅ two drawn pages | ▶ **Two REAL mini-pages, same photo, same colours** — one parallaxes under the cursor, one is dead still. Four grey bars cannot show motion because there is nothing to move. This is the control he named. |
| H2 photos vs facts | ✅ two drawn pages | ▶ Same real photograph and same real price list, arranged two ways. |
| H3 dark or light | ✅ two drawn pages | ▶ Same page, two grounds, real type. |
| **NEW corners** | — | ▶ **His idea.** Two identical cards, 16px radius against 0. One tap, and it is the most visible single decision in a design system. |
| **NEW type** | — | ▶ Their own business name set two ways — heavy condensed caps, plain lowercase grotesque. Decides more character than colour does. |
| **NEW buttons** | — | ▶ Solid block against outline. Changes how pushy the whole page feels. |
| **NEW photo treatment** | — | ▶ Full-bleed edge-to-edge against a photo inside a bordered card. |
| **NEW page width** | — | ▶ Edge to edge against a centred column. |
| **NEW gallery** | — | ▶ A grid of stills against a drag-the-slider before/after. Detailers have strong views and no words for it. |
| **NEW header** | — | ▶ A bar that follows you down the page, or one that stays at the top. Show it scrolling. |
| H6 serious vs friendly | three chips | ▶ **The same sentence written both ways, using their own name.** *"Concours-level detailing, by appointment"* against *"We'll make your car look unreal."* An adjective is abstract; a headline is not. |
| H5 colours | ✅ picker + hex + many | ▶ Add: **pull the colours out of their logo or van photo** and offer them as swatches. Most detailers have a colour and have never written it as a number. |

## 9 · Questions still worth adding

Beyond the seven new visual either/ors in §1.

1. **Who are your customers, mostly?** Daily drivers, enthusiasts, dealerships,
   fleets. Changes the photographs and the words. *In the original short intake
   and dropped building the long one — my mistake.*
2. **What do people ask you before they book?** Three or four, and they are the
   FAQ. **44% of real sites have one.** Also dropped, also a mistake.
3. **One page, or a page per service?**
4. **Should your opening hours be on the site?**
5. **A tips-and-advice section?** It earns local search traffic and it is a
   commitment to writing, so it has to be asked rather than assumed.
6. **Is same-day work something to advertise?**
7. **Whose name is the business in — yours, or a trading name?** Decides the
   footer, the legal line and the email address.
