## The answer

### The software is not the constraint. His birthday is.

**He turns 18 on 2 December 2026.** He has decided to wait rather than route the
business through his dad, which is the right call at three months' distance.
**So the earliest he can charge a customer is 2 December**, no matter how
finished the code is.

**Add roughly a week for the setup steps** — EIN (same day), Stripe account
opened and activated (a few days for verification), business bank account, the
Lakewood licence.

> ## First sales call: the week of 8 December 2026.
> **Set by his 18th birthday, not by the build.**

### When the software itself will be done

**18–27 sessions of work remain.** Three honest scenarios:

| | Pace | Software finished |
|---|---|---|
| **If the last eleven days continue** | ~1 session a day | **late September** |
| **Realistic once school and detailing take their share** | 3–4 sessions a week | **mid-to-late October** |
| **Slow stretch — exams, a busy detailing month** | 1–2 sessions a week | **late November** |

**Every one of those lands before 2 December.** The middle scenario finishes
with **five to six weeks of slack**, and even the slow one gets there.

### What to do with the slack, because it is worth more than it looks

**Phase 5 — put his own detailing business on the platform and run it in
parallel.** It is already in the plan (5.1, 5.2, 5.3), it needs no legal setup,
it costs nothing, and **it is the single best bug-finder available**: real
customers, real bookings, real money, on the real product, every day.

**He asked not to count his own testing, and that is fair — but the gap between
"software done" and "legally able to sell" is going to exist regardless.**
Spending it running his own business on the thing means that on 8 December he is
selling a product he has used daily for six weeks, with a month of real bookings
behind it. **That is worth more on a cold call than any feature left on this
list.**

## What could break this estimate

**Named honestly, because an estimate without them is decoration.**

1. **Phase 3 is the real unknown.** Bespoke per-client websites have never been
   built here, the owner rejected the shared-template version, and **3.1 is an
   approval gate on him.** If it turns out to need more than the engine plus a
   brief, it is the item that moves the date.
2. **The pace so far is not a school-year pace.** Eleven consecutive days with
   no gaps, alongside a detailing business making ~$2,000 a month, is a sprint.
   The middle scenario already assumes it slows by two thirds.
3. **Owner gates stall the plan by design.** 3.1 needs his approval, 7.1 needs
   his legal text, and every day one of those waits is a day the estimate moves.
4. **The discovery rate has not slowed.** The last eleven days turned up a live
   white-screen crash on `main`, an invoice that never added up, eleven email
   headlines under the contrast floor, and a booking page that had been
   double-bookable in one configuration. **There is no reason to think the next
   eleven find nothing**, and none of that is on the list above.
5. **The first client site is not on this list.** Building a website for the
   first real customer is per-customer work that starts *after* the sale, and
   the plan says the kit builds it. **How long that takes is unmeasured**, and
   it sits between the first sale and the first happy customer.

## How to keep this honest

**Re-run the counts rather than trusting this file.** Commits per day:

```
git log --format="%ad" --date=short | sort | uniq -c
```

Items closed and remaining: `grep -c "^- \[x\]" docs/roadmap.md` and
`grep -c "^- \[ \]" docs/roadmap.md`.

**Both numbers were 28 and 23 on 4 September 2026.** If the open count is not
falling by roughly one every day or two, the middle scenario is not the one
happening, and the date should be re-stated rather than hoped about.
