    The Thread

    Your Saturday already exists — it is just scattered across a text thread. As you scroll, each message detaches from the phone and lands as a row in the dashboard: "u free sat?" becomes 9:00 — Marcus Hill, Wash & Wax, $95. Nothing is deleted and replaced; it is sorted.   1. Direction 1

    The Seam

    A detailer sells the edge between dirty and clean, so that edge is the layout. One seam, used at three sizes: down the hero photo, across the booking progress, and up the side of a job that's underway.

    Feels like a well-lit workshop at night. Dark, precise, quiet. Type Archivo and nothing else, stretched and squeezed across its width. Motion the seam slides once on load, then follows your mouse.

    Risk: dark can read cold to a small-business owner, and this is the one closest in spirit to the look you just threw out.

  2. Direction 2

    Showroom

    We sell a website, so the page shows the website instead of describing it. Every claim is a picture of the real thing with one line under it. The background alternates between light and dark all the way down, which is the single strongest thing you pointed at in your own references.

    Feels like a good trade magazine. Bright, blunt, confident. Type Anton, very heavy, against a light text face. Motion sections start moving before they reach you, then stop. Nothing else.

    Risk: the loudest of the four. Red and heavy caps are a strong flavour to look at every day.

  3. Direction 3

    Ticket

    Booking a detail is buying a ticket, so the thing the customer ends up holding is designed as an object, with a punched edge and a stub. That punched edge then becomes the system everywhere else.

    Feels like a conditioned leather seat. Warm, generous, friendly. Type Petrona, a warm serif run from hairline to black. Motion one staggered load, the ticket dropping in last, slightly askew.

    Risk: the biggest leap from anything you've said you like. If you hate it in two seconds, that is useful information.

  4. Direction 4

    Approach

    Your idea, built: the car changes from dull to corrected as you scroll the hero. Everything that could have been spent on decoration was spent on that instead. There is no brand colour anywhere on this page. The only colour comes out of the photographs.

    Feels like an Apple product page. Almost nothing on screen, and it moves a lot. Type Onest, one family, hairline against black. Motion the most of the four, and it says on screen exactly how long it holds you: 1.4 screens.

    Risk: with no brand colour at all, this is the one that most depends on good photography, and it is nearest to the bakery page you disliked, which also ran on two colours.

To see these on your phone — run python -m http.server 8080 --directory docs/design-directions from the repo folder, leave the window open, then on your phone (same wifi) open http://192.168.0.126:8080/. The phone is the test that matters for direction 5. Two things only your phone can answer: whether the pinned section where the messages fly feels smooth or stuttery, and whether the weighted scroll is nicer than the normal one — ?smooth=0 turns it off so you can feel both in one sitting. ?lite=1 shows the page with every animation switched off, which is what someone who has asked their phone to reduce motion sees.
