01 · the pair

A poster that has to hold a catalogue.

Both references were loaded and measured on 9 September, not remembered.

Reference A — the look

“Professional Detailing Website Design”, DamiraDovlatova. A studio concept: one screen, red on black, a heavy italic headline at poster size over a full-bleed car photograph inside one big round-cornered panel, tiny all-caps micro-labels, a glass product card, a hanging tab.

Reference B — the content

carolinamobilecarwash.com

A real working detailer in Summerville, SC. Twenty-two services across eight vehicle types — bus, RV, golf cart, semi, boat, fleet — per-foot pricing, monthly plans, fourteen towns, and the deposit / weather / no-rush policies nobody puts on a site.

the tension, and it has a wrong answer

A poster has no catalogue in it and a catalogue has no poster in it. Resolution: the poster is the first screen and never returns. From the second screen down the page is a depot board — the sign bolted to a yard wall listing everything they will wash and what it costs. The join between the two is the vehicle switch below.

02 · colour

Near-black lacquer, one loud red, one light band.

Every ratio below is computed by this page against its own rendered ground and printed beside the swatch. A figure typed by hand goes stale the first time a token moves, and this repo has shipped that fault five times. Tokens with transparency are composited before measuring — the raw value of a 10% white is a false pass at 1.00:1.

Two tokens for one red, on purpose. A red that passes with white on it as a button fill does not pass as small red text on near-black. And a hairline and a control's edge are two different things — decoration has no floor, an edge needs 3:1.

04 · corners

Round, and the only square thing is a price.

The exact inversion of site 4, which was 2px everywhere with one round badge. This is the most visible single difference between the two sites and it is deliberate.

28px

Panels, the hero, the switchboard.

16px

Cards, vehicle tiles, the glass card.

10px

Inputs and small controls.

999px

Buttons and chips. Capsules stay capsules.

0px

The price cell, and nothing else.

not used

The corner we are NOT using: a small 4px radius. It is neither soft nor sharp, and a page that mixes it with 28px reads as two corner languages.

05 · ground

Lacquer, and the page changes ground twice.

His own note: “a very plain colored black background… maybe a gradient, a little pattern, something just to make it feel more professional.” The ground here is what a red car's flank does under a lamp — a wide red bloom low-left, a cold bloom top-right, a vertical falloff and a fine grain. Four layers, one fixed element, no loop. The whole page you are reading is standing on it.

and this is the light band

Black band to white band.

All five sites he sent unprompted switch ground between sections, and not one of my five pages did. Two sections take this treatment — ceramic coating and the monthly plan — and everything else is black. A new ground means every ratio has to be taken again, which is why the band's own ink is a separate token measured separately.

Carried in and not re-argued: anything that opens ships its exit in the same change; nothing animates twice; a swap is never a direct child of the arrival container; the hidden state of a reveal is added by script, never by the stylesheet, or a failed script is a blank page.

08 · the one effect borrowed from the references

The gloss travels as you scroll.

The Dribbble panel's whole character is specular — a lit red surface. This takes that literally: a highlight band, cut at the same −12° as the type, dragged across the panel by one scroll progress number. Scroll this page and watch it; scroll back up and it runs backwards, because there is only the one number and no second animation was written.

progress 0.00
scroll-linked, no timer, no library. The band is 34% wide and travels −60% → 220%.
09 · the signature device

The vehicle switchboard.

This is the join between the poster and the catalogue, and it is the single most transferable thing in the Carolina reference: that site segments by what the vehicle IS, not by what size it is. Every site in this set so far ladders small / midsize / large. Press a tile.

The heading and the sentence above do not move. Four labels drifting at four heights is a scatter — in a swap, the content changes and the frame holds still. The rows rise 8px on a 20ms stagger, capped at 160ms, and furniture opts out.

10 · photography

Nineteen were rendered and looked at. Eleven survived.

Alt text does not mention badges, so every candidate goes to a contact sheet and gets looked at. Two were rejected for exactly that, and a third for being a motorhome that a caption called a van.

Photographs are cropped to 16:10 or 4:3, desaturated ~8% so the red of the page stays the loudest red on screen, and always inside a 16px or 28px corner. Never a grey placeholder box.

11 · the rules

Ten things, each one paid for.

  1. The italic is the brand. Display leans; nothing else does.
  2. Red is a fill, never small text on the dark ground. Two tokens, because one red cannot do both jobs.
  3. 28px corners, and the only square thing on the page is a price cell.
  4. A hairline and a control's edge are two tokens. Decoration has no floor; an edge needs 3:1.
  5. The page changes ground twice — black, light, black. Every ratio is taken again on the new ground.
  6. Segment by what the vehicle IS, not by what size it is. That is the whole reason this site does not look like the other four.
  7. No footer wordmark. It is on six of the twenty-eight pages here and all six are sites 1–4. A device on that many is a house tell.
  8. No marquee band, even though Carolina has one, for the same reason: it is already on sites 1, 2 and 3.
  9. Every price shown here is an example's number. A real tenant's ladder comes from the platform, never from the file.
  10. The booking form is ours, in this skin. A bespoke form may ask; it may never compute a price or decide a free slot.
