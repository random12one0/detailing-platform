01 — where this comes from

The two references disagree. That is the point.

Neither was described from memory. Both were opened and their computed styles read, because every feel this project guessed at was rejected and every one it measured was kept.

Chicago Auto Pros

Square: 11 elements at 1px, and the only round thing on the page is a 50% icon badge, twenty of them. Heavy: headline 60px/700, and the body is 15px/600. Royal blue, 31 uses. Every seam a diagonal.

auxia.io

Round: pills, 16px panels, 12 and 8 inside them. One weight — 500, everywhere, from 13px to 96px. Bone ground. Electric blue: 7 solid uses and 36 at 20% alpha, which is all the line-work.

The resolution

Chicago's corners, weight and density; auxia's ground, line-work and technical labels. Their one real conflict — diagonal seams against right-angle routing — is settled by running the schematic on the bias, so both are one gesture at two scales.

An accent used as a fill and an accent used as text are two tokens.

Found by measuring, on this sheet. #0B4FFF carries white at 5.87:1 as a button, and is 2.81:1 as a headline word on graphite. The lighter #5C8BFF is the reverse — 5.19:1 as text, 3.19:1 under white as a fill. One token cannot be both, so there are two.

Two border widths, and they are different things

A decorative hairline is 1px at low alpha. The edge of something you type in or press is 1.5px and must clear 3:1 — a control whose edge you cannot see is not a control.

Depth is the slant and the overlap

One shadow exists, and only on a panel that overlaps a photograph. Everywhere else the page gets its depth from the diagonal seam and from things sitting on top of other things.

05 — the seam

Every seam is a cut, at one angle.

Chicago's signature, measured: photograph and panel meet on a slant and the side flips each row. One variable — --slant — and it flattens to zero below 768px, because a diagonal seam across a 392px column is a wedge of dead space rather than a gesture.

The weighted scroll — ported, not written

WHEEL 1.22 · LERP 0.055, lifted from our own landing page, constants included. A version written from scratch used 0.115 with no wheel multiplier and read as a lurch and a dead stop. It moves the real scroll position rather than a wrapper, so sticky elements keep working; it is off on touch, where a finger already gets it right; and the stylesheet cannot carry scroll-behavior: smooth, because the browser would be animating toward a target this moves every frame.

08 — the one taken from the references

A line that draws itself as you scroll, on the bias.

auxia routes its page along a hairline that fills with the scroll. Chicago cuts every seam on a diagonal. This is both: the same line, running at the page's own angle, lighting each stage as it reaches it. Scroll slowly through it — every part of the section is glued to the scroll, and nothing in it plays on a timer.

Two layers, travelling against each other

The photograph sinks while the panel on it climbs. The sign is the effect: same speed in the same direction is one layer. And the limit is approached, never hit — off = cap × (1 − e−raw/cap), so a layer still moves on the first pixel and never changes speed in a single frame. A hard clamp makes it stop dead, and that is visible immediately.

09 — layout and spacing

1080 wide, an 8px scale, and the phone is a re-layout.

Scale

2 · 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 40 · 64 · 96. Nothing between.

Widths swept

1920 · 1440 · 768 · 392 · 320. 392 is the real customer; 320 is the floor the product promises.

What changes on a phone

The slant flattens to 0°. The routed line goes vertical down the left gutter. A bottom dock appears. Rotating the phone changes nothing.

10 — the rules a builder cannot break

Ten things, each one paid for.

1 · An animation that plays once is a bug

Leaving the viewport in either direction re-arms the element.

2 · Reveals come from the scroll, not an observer

IntersectionObserver does not fire in some embedded browsers, and because the hidden state is added by script, an observer that never fires is a blank page.

3 · Never take the wheel away

Weight comes from what the page's own layers do. A lerped hijack was built once and rejected on sight.

4 · If one part of a section is scroll-linked, all of it is

A line that fills with the scroll beside headings that fade on arrival is the seam that reads as glitchy.

5 · A row arrives together

Stagger down a page, never across a row. A per-card delay on four cards in one row is a queue, and a queue is what “delayed” means.

6 · Every contrast figure is computed, then written

Off the rendered page, sampling inside the fill with the glyphs painted out. Never off the token table.

7 · Every heading is a label

If it would work as a band name it is wrong. No heading counts anything — counts go stale the moment the section grows.

8 · Delete any sentence that adds no fact

And never sell the baseline: “we come to you” is the trade, not a reason to book.

9 · A photograph above the fold is a claim about the fold

Measure it at 900px tall, not in the file.

10 · Proof is a different shape on every site

Rating / years / cars-done, in that order, is banned — it was recognised across three sites at a glance.
