## 2. What this says about the three pages he rejected

**All three of those were serif-led, warm-paper, editorially restrained.** Not
one of these five is. There is **no serif display face anywhere in the set**,
no muted palette, no letterspaced small-caps label as a structural device, and
nothing that reads as a catalogue or a document.

**And two of my five 2026-09-07 pages went the same wrong way.** `g-estate`
(card stock, a numbered catalogue, Bitter) and `d-ridgeline` (warm off-black,
Newsreader, a rate table read as a logbook) are further from this set than
anything else I built. **`f-sudsy` — painted yellow, price as the headline,
Archivo at scale — is the closest thing in the repo to what he has just sent**,
and `e-kiln` is second on grounds of scale and photography while being far too
austere.

## 3. The five things all five share — and the pages he rejected did not do

**1 · A REAL CAR PHOTOGRAPH IS THE HERO, AT SCALE.** Five out of five. Cut out
and overlapping type (A), full-bleed (B, E), or inside a giant rounded card
(C). Glossy, lit, recent. **Never an abstract ground, an illustration or a
texture.**

**2 · THE HEADLINE IS HUGE, HEAVY AND USUALLY CONDENSED CAPS.** A, C and D all
set a word at 100–200px and let it collide with or sit behind the photograph.
B and E are quieter but still large and still sans. **The display face is
always a grotesque or a condensed grotesque. Never a serif.**

**3 · ONE SATURATED ACCENT, USED LOUDLY.** Blue (A), red (B), red (C), orange
(D). Buttons, numbers, eyebrow labels, one whole hero card. Nothing muted,
nothing "sophisticated", no second accent.

**4 · THE PAGE SWITCHES GROUND BETWEEN SECTIONS.** Black band, white band,
black band. A, B and D all do it; it is the main compositional device in B.
**None of my five pages did this** — each committed to one ground throughout,
which reads as more tasteful and less alive.

**5 · NUMBERS, EVERYWHERE, AS DECORATION AS MUCH AS INFORMATION.** 7K / 3K /
5K / 8K, 24+ / 1.2K+ / 35 / 99%, 4.9, 4.6, 150k, 12,025, 250,000, 10,000.
Four of five carry a stat strip. **This is the single easiest thing to copy and
the one a real detailer can genuinely fill in.**

Two more worth having, present in three of five: **a real price table or
package cards with actual figures**, and **testimonials with a star rating**.
A's table is tabbed BY VEHICLE TYPE, which is exactly the ladder this product
already computes.

## 4. THE UNCOMFORTABLE PART, AND IT IS THE MOST USEFUL LINE IN THIS FILE

**Several of these sites are close to the "2026 slop" recipe that
`docs/design-knowledge.md` warns about.** Three-up service cards, a stats
strip, a pricing table, an FAQ accordion, testimonials with faces, a logo row.
B has four of the six in order.

**He likes them anyway, and his taste is the requirement.** So the anti-slop
research has to be read for what it actually is: **a guard against the
GENERIC-AI version of a commercial page, not an argument for an unusual one.**
What separates these five from a slop page is not structure. It is:

- photography that is real, specific and expensive-looking
- type set two or three sizes bigger than feels safe
- one colour committed to
- and enough contrast between sections that the page has a pulse

**That is craft and confidence, not novelty.** A session that reads "avoid the
centred hero, the three cards and the pricing toggle" and therefore builds a
catalogue on card stock has followed the letter and produced exactly the thing
he has now rejected three times.

The never-defaults still stand as never-defaults: Inter, Roboto, system-ui,
Space Grotesk, purple-on-white gradients, copy that explains what the label
already said. **Nothing in this file relaxes those.**

### FOUR THINGS THEY TAUGHT THAT ARE NOT IN § 3

**1 · ALT TEXT DOES NOT MENTION BADGES, AND A CONTACT SHEET COSTS ONE MINUTE.**
Site j's first hero was chosen from a description reading *"man in blue crew
neck t-shirt standing beside black car"*. It is a **Bugatti**, and the
detailer's shirt carries another company's mark legibly. On a one-van mobile
page in Columbus that contradicts the only claim the page makes, and the shirt
is the same defect site 2 already shipped. **Render every candidate to a
contact sheet and look at it before choosing one.** Doing that also caught a
Range Rover wordmark across a grille and a Lamborghini caliper badge in the
existing photo bank.

**2 · A CONTRAST COMMENT IS WRITTEN AFTER THE RUN, NEVER BEFORE.** All three
pages had their ratios typed from judgement first, and on all three the
calculator disagreed with every figure. Two were genuine failures under a
comment claiming a pass: i's form-input border at **1.93:1** where it claimed
3.06, and k's quiet text on the paint at **4.41:1** where it claimed 4.55.
That is site 1's defect twice more, in the two pages written to avoid it.

**3 · A DECORATIVE HAIRLINE AND A CONTROL'S EDGE ARE TWO TOKENS.** All three
pages now carry `--edge*` (3:1, borders of things you type in or press) beside
`--rule*` (quiet, separating rows and cards). One token for both is how a form
ends up outlined at 1.29:1.

**4 · "A PHOTOGRAPH AT SCALE" IS A CLAIM ABOUT THE FOLD, NOT THE MARKUP.**
k's headline ran to five lines and pushed its photograph to 815px down a 900px
screen: the rule was true of the file and false of what anybody saw. Measured
on all three now — the first photograph starts at 0px, 239px and 616px against
a 900px fold.

## 5. What to do with it

**Wait for his better batch before building.** He said so in the same breath as
sending these, and a sixth attempt built on a first reading of a partial set is
the fourth guess this repo has already recorded twice as expensive.

When the rest arrive: add them to § 1, re-derive § 3 across the whole set, and
only then write a brief. The brief still needs what
`docs/tenant-site-briefs-2026-09-07.md` § 5 measured as the thing that actually
produces variety — **a TENSION sentence and a one-noun answer to "what IS this
page"** — but it now has a house style to resolve those tensions INSIDE, which
is what was missing.
